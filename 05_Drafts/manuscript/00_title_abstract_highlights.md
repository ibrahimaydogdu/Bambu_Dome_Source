# Title (working title)

Is Bamboo Truly Zero-Carbon? Single-Objective Structural Optimization and the Decarbonization Paradox of Large-Span Spatial Domes

## Abstract

This study sizes three Guadua bamboo space domes (spans 16, 32 and 40 m; 115, 456 and 1464 members) and accounts for the carbon of their connection steel and grout. Allowable stress design follows the IStructE Manual to ISO 22156:2021 and NSR-10. The objective is culm weight, with a multiplicative penalty for violated constraints; each design group takes one of 24 hollow circular sections. The artificial bee colony (ABC), ant colony optimization (ACO) and dandelion optimizer (DO) were run with 30 seeds per dome, 270 runs, all feasible. The lightest designs weigh 5.800, 41.058 and 66.352 kN. ABC had the lowest mean rank on every dome (Friedman p ≤ 4.91 × 10⁻⁹) and was lighter than ACO and DO (Holm-adjusted p ≤ 5.53 × 10⁻⁴). Embodied carbon (A1–A3, no biogenic carbon) was computed afterwards. Connection steel and grout account for 74.1–82.1% of the baseline carbon; changing one parameter at a time gives 41.1–93.9%, with the largest effect from the emission factor of the culm. For the same geometry, the bamboo members weigh 0.51–0.68 of the steel members and the bamboo systems carry 0.25–0.35 of the steel carbon at baseline factors, but the bamboo systems weigh more than the steel systems. The material cost ratio of bamboo to steel is 0.61–1.39 across four price scenarios and diameter exponents α = 1.5–3. Among the feasible designs visited, the lightest design has 1.6–6.7% more carbon than the lowest carbon found. The results hold for these three models and the stated assumptions.
