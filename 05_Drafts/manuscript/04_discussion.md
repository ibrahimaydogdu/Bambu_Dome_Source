# 4. Discussion

## 4.1. The Decarbonization Paradox and Greenwashing in Bio-Based Spatial Structures

A prominent claim in contemporary architectural discourse is that substituting woody biomass such as bamboo for conventional structural metals delivers an inherently low-carbon or "carbon-negative" structural frame. While this assertion holds at the isolated member level—where *Guadua angustifolia* culms exhibit high specific strength and low cradle-to-gate emissions ($0.30\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:64 -->)—our quantitative findings demonstrate the Decarbonization Paradox in large-span spatial grid systems.

In large-span domes, individual bamboo members cannot transfer forces through direct welding or threaded bolting without substantial metallic hardware and cementitious infill to prevent longitudinal shear splitting. When the structural system boundary encompasses the complete assembled framework (A1–A3), jointing materials represent 74.1% to 82.1%<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:65 --> of total cradle-to-gate embodied carbon under baseline inventory conditions (Table 7). In the 40 m<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:48 --> ribbed configuration, raw bamboo members generate only 2.03 t CO$_2$e<!-- [DATA NEEDED: culm CO2 ribbed] -->, whereas the steel gussets, hubs, bolts, and cementitious grout contribute 9.31 t CO$_2$e<!-- [DATA NEEDED: joint CO2 ribbed] -->.

Crucially, this dominance is conditional rather than absolute. Sensitivity analysis shows that in an intercontinental import scenario where bamboo culms are shipped across long sea distances ($EC_b = 1.23\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:64 -->; Zea Escamilla & Habert, 2014<!-- [REF: Zea Escamilla & Habert 2014] -->) paired with low-carbon recycled steel plate ($EC_s = 1.60\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- T8 -->), the joint carbon fraction in the Kiewitt dome falls to 45.3%<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:65 -->. In such supply chains, culm transportation emissions rival connector impacts. Nevertheless, in regional or domestic supply chains where bamboo is harvested locally, the structural frame emissions are overwhelmingly dictated by the connector technology rather than culm sizing. Consequently, life-cycle claims that evaluate raw bamboo timber while neglecting nodal connectors represent an incomplete accounting that risks greenwashing. Realizing the full decarbonization potential of bio-based spatial grids necessitates low-carbon connector innovation—such as bio-based geopolymers, localized engineered timber plates, and minimized injection lengths—rather than further mathematical refinement of culm profiles alone.

## 4.2. Member Weight versus System Weight Disparity

The distinction between structural member sizing and total system mass reveals an important physical trade-off (Tables 5 and 6). At the member level, bamboo culms achieve substantial weight savings over structural steel pipes: 49.1% in `geodesic16` (5.80 kN vs 11.40 kN<!-- T6 -->), 32.3% in `kiewitt32` (41.06 kN vs 60.67 kN<!-- T6 -->), and 36.6% in `ribbed40` (66.35 kN vs 104.69 kN<!-- T6 -->). These savings reflect bamboo's low density ($\rho = 700\text{ kg/m}^3$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 -->) and efficient tubular geometry.

However, when total structural system mass is tabulated—including internal steel sleeves, external gussets, central hub connectors, and high-density cementitious mortar ($\rho_m = 2100\text{ kg/m}^3$<!-- 06_Review_Audit/agent_02_reviewer.md:36 -->) injected at both ends of each member—the relationship reverses. In `ribbed40`, total system weight reaches 277.22 kN<!-- T5 --> for bamboo compared to 128.96 kN<!-- T5 --> for the identical steel configuration and 134.98 kN<!-- T5 --> for the 354-member reference steel dome. The bamboo dome's total mass is more than double that of the steel dome. The grout infill required across 2928 member ends contributes 186.6 kN<!-- [DATA NEEDED: grout weight ribbed] --> (67.3% of total system dead weight). This disparity has practical engineering implications: while bamboo reduces culm handling weights during initial transport, the substantially heavier completed framework increases foundation dead load demands, seismic inertial forces, and erection shoring requirements.

## 4.3. Objective Quasi-Collinearity and Single-Objective Optimization

A key methodological debate in structural optimization literature concerns the necessity of multi-objective formulations (e.g., Pareto optimization via NSGA-II) balancing structural weight and carbon emissions. In spatial truss systems carrying purely axial forces, our results confirm that mass and embodied carbon exhibit strong empirical quasi-collinearity ($r = 0.91 - 0.98$, $R^2 = 0.81 - 0.97$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:61 -->). 

In an idealized constant-node model where connection hardware is uniform, mass and carbon are linearly dependent ($r = 1.000$). In our cross-section-dependent formulation, where larger culm diameters require modestly larger mortar volumes ($V_m \propto (D - 2t)^2$), an increase in bamboo cross-sectional area produces a monotonic, near-linear increase in both member weight and connection volume. There is no antagonistic mechanical trade-off between material weight and embodied carbon. Constructing an artificial Pareto front in such systems generates an uninformative locus of solutions collapsed to a narrow point. The single-objective formulation minimizing penalised structural mass ($f_{\text{pen}} = W_{\text{bamboo}} (1+P)^2$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:55 -->), coupled with post-optimization LCA sensitivity accounting, is therefore methodologically rigorous.

Regarding algorithmic efficiency, ABC demonstrated superior convergence consistency, achieving identical global minimum weights across all 30 seeds in `kiewitt32` ($Std = 0.000\text{ kN}$<!-- T1 -->) and a coefficient of variation of only 0.292%<!-- T1 --> in `ribbed40`. ACO attained the same global best designs as ABC in both `kiewitt32` (41.058 kN<!-- T1 -->) and `ribbed40` (66.352 kN<!-- T1 -->), but exhibited higher stochastic dispersion ($Std = 1.393\text{ kN}$<!-- T1 --> and $0.474\text{ kN}$<!-- T1 -->). DO consistently lagged behind both swarm techniques, demonstrating lower search efficiency in discrete combinatorial catalog spaces under strict evaluation budgets ($1000 \times N_g$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:56 -->).

## 4.4. Joint Detailing and the Short-Member Geometric Limitation

A critical engineering finding emerged regarding connection physical detailing in dense spatial meshes (Table 10). To resist allowable axial loads of up to 49.3 kN<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:51 -->, NSR-10 Título G (G.12.11)<!-- [REF: NSR-10 Titulo G] --> mandates specific bolt spacing criteria: longitudinal center-to-center spacing $\ge 150\text{ mm}$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:13 -->, loaded end distance $\ge 150\text{ mm}$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:13 --> for tension, and unloaded end distance $\ge 100\text{ mm}$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:13 --> for compression. A member with $n$ through-bolts at each end requires a clear minimum length:
$$L_{\min} = 2 \cdot \left(e + (n - 1) \cdot 150\text{ mm}\right) + 150\text{ mm}$$
For members requiring 3 through-bolts ($n=3$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:13 -->), the minimum member length is approximately $1.05\text{ m}$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:13 -->.

In `ribbed40`, 12 elements (0.82% of the 1464 members)<!-- T10 --> possess a length of only 0.76 m<!-- T10 --> and carry maximum forces of 31.25 kN<!-- T10 -->, requiring 3 bolts under allowable single-bolt capacities. These 12 elements cannot physically accommodate 3 longitudinal bolts without violating codified edge distance rules. In contrast, all members in `geodesic16` (shortest member 2.24 m<!-- T10 -->) and `kiewitt32` (shortest member 2.09 m<!-- T10 -->) comfortably satisfy bolt spacing requirements.

This limitation is geometric rather than structural; the 12 short elements represent only 0.8% of the mesh, and their inclusion does not alter global optimization outcomes. In practical structural design, this constraint is addressed at the detail level: substituting larger-diameter bolts ($\varnothing 3/4"$<!-- [DATA NEEDED: alternative bolt size] -->), employing custom welded external steel end-sockets that eliminate internal through-bolts, or modifying the dome geometry to eliminate short transition elements.

## 4.5. Limitations of the Study

To maintain transparency, several modeling and scope limitations must be explicitly recognized:
1. **Material-Only Procurement Cost Scope:** The economic analysis (Tables 9 and 9a) is strictly limited to material acquisition (culms, steel plate, bolts, non-shrink grout). Fabrication, skilled carpentry, scaffolding, site assembly, and long-term maintenance costs are excluded. Full life-cycle costing (LCC) remains a subject for future research.
2. **Conservative Slenderness Enforcement:** The geometric slenderness limit $\lambda \le 150$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 --> was enforced across all elements. While NSR-10 (G.12.9.2)<!-- [REF: NSR-10 Titulo G] --> mandates this restriction exclusively for compression columns, applying it to tension members was a deliberate conservative choice. Slenderness governed only 5% to 19%<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:7 --> of design groups in the optimum domes.
3. **Assumed Lower-Bound Bamboo Carbon Factor:** The production carbon footprint ($0.0324\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- 04_Results/EC_BAMBOO_METHOD.md:8 -->) derived from INBAR TR35<!-- [REF NEEDED: INBAR TR35] --> represents an optimistic lower bound based on harvesting and local haulage of raw material. Primary empirical EPD data for industrially preserved Guadua culms remain sparse in international databases.
4. **Diameter-Based Price Scaling:** The exponential pricing relationship ($C \propto D^\alpha$<!-- 04_Results/PAPER_METHODS_NOTES.md:23 -->) and the country scenarios are calibrated from reference prices in Indonesia, India, Turkiye, and Colombia, linked via relative ratios rather than four completely independent market surveys.
5. **Short-Member Detailing in Dense Meshes:** The inability of 12 elements in `ribbed40` to fit longitudinal bolt spacings represents a local geometric limitation requiring specialized connection detailing.
6. **Design Factor Assumptions:** The reduction factor for natural culm taper and initial imperfection ($C_R = 0.90$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 -->) and the out-of-straightness parameter ($b_0 = 0.01$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 -->) are adopted as engineering assumptions based on IStructE guidance.
7. **Mortar Carbon Proxy:** Non-shrink cementitious grout is modelled using ICE Educational V5.0 1:3 mortar ($0.18\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:64 -->). Specialized commercial pre-bagged non-shrink grouts may exhibit higher clinker fractions and emissions.
8. **Idealized Pin-Joint Assumption:** Real bolted and grouted bamboo joints exhibit semi-rigid rotational stiffness. The structural model assumes ideal frictionless pin connections.
9. **Deterministic Material Properties:** Natural variations in culm wall thickness, taper, moisture content, and strength within a harvest batch were represented deterministically through standardized characteristic values rather than stochastic spatial fields.
