# 1. Introduction

## 1.1. Motivation: Decarbonizing Long-Span Spatial Roofs

The building and construction sector is responsible for approximately 37% of global energy-related greenhouse gas emissions, with structural framing materials—principally concrete and structural steel—accounting for the majority of embodied carbon (A1–A3) in commercial and public infrastructure. In large-span architectural structures such as sports arenas, exhibition centers, and transit terminals, single-layer and double-layer spatial grids are widely selected for their structural efficiency and high stiffness-to-weight ratios. However, conventional space structures fabricated from welded or bolted structural steel pipes consume substantial quantities of refined steel, generating significant embodied emissions during raw material processing and manufacturing.

Fast-growing woody bamboo species, particularly *Guadua angustifolia* Kunth native to Latin America, have emerged as viable bio-based structural alternatives to conventional construction materials. Structural bamboo culms naturally form efficient hollow circular sections with dense longitudinal sclerenchyma fibers concentrated near the outer wall, yielding favorable specific strength and stiffness properties. Unlike industrial steel sections that entail energy-intensive smelting at temperatures exceeding 1400 °C, bamboo culms mature in 4 to 5 years, sequester atmospheric carbon during cultivation, and require minimal processing—principally harvesting, preservative boron borate immersion, and air seasoning. At the raw material level, processed round culms exhibit a low cradle-to-gate embodied carbon footprint of approximately $0.30\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e/kg}$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:64 -->. Consequently, substituting bamboo culms for structural steel members in spatial grids offers an appealing strategy for structural decarbonization.

## 1.2. Structural Mechanics, Buckling, and Modern Design Provisions

Despite favorable mechanical properties, applying natural bamboo to large-span spatial domes presents distinct structural engineering challenges:
1. **Geometric Instability and Column Buckling:** Bamboo culms are slender, thin-walled biological tubes susceptible to flexural Euler buckling under axial compression. Natural culms exhibit cross-sectional out-of-roundness, wall thickness variations along their length, and initial out-of-straightness. Accurately sizing compressive members requires non-linear column stability formulations.
2. **Standardization and Design Codes:** Historically, bamboo engineering relied on empirical rules or local codes with fragmented safety factors. In 2021, the International Organization for Standardization published ISO 22156:2021 (*Bamboo structures — Structural design*)<!-- [REF: ISO 22156:2021] -->, followed by the publication of the comprehensive *Manual for the design of bamboo structures to ISO 22156:2021* by the Institution of Structural Engineers (IStructE; Trujillo et al., 2025<!-- [REF: Trujillo et al. 2025] -->). These standards establish an Allowable Stress Design (ASD) framework incorporating load duration factors ($C_{DF}$), imperfection factors ($C_R$), and Ylinen's generalized column buckling equation.
3. **Connection Demands and Splitting Susceptibility:** Bamboo culms exhibit low transverse tensile and shear strength parallel to the fibers (shear strength typically 2 to 4 MPa). Direct bolted connections without reinforcement cause premature longitudinal splitting at member ends. In engineering practice, this limitation is overcome using hybrid joints comprising internal or external steel gussets, through-bolts, and non-shrink cementitious grout injected into the internodes to distribute concentrated bearing stresses (Trujillo et al., 2025<!-- [REF: Trujillo et al. 2025] -->; Correal et al., 2021<!-- [REF: Correal et al. 2021] -->).

## 1.3. Literature Gap and the Decarbonization Paradox

A review of structural optimization and life cycle assessment (LCA) literature reveals three critical knowledge gaps:
1. **Absence of Codified Metaheuristic Sizing for Bamboo Spatial Grids:** While metaheuristic algorithms (e.g., Genetic Algorithms, Particle Swarm Optimization, Harmony Search) have been extensively applied to steel spatial trusses (e.g., Saka, 1991<!-- [REF NEEDED: Saka 1991] -->; Hasançebi et al., 2009<!-- [REF NEEDED: Hasançebi et al. 2009] -->), structural optimization studies on bamboo structures remain largely restricted to small-scale planar frames, trusses, or conceptual shells. Systematic discrete sizing optimization of 40 m<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:48 --> spatial dome grids across catalog profiles under ISO 22156:2021 ASD provisions has not been previously reported.
2. **Methodological Misapplication of Multi-Objective Pareto Optimality:** In structural optimization literature, researchers frequently deploy multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (such as NSGA-II) to formulate simultaneous minimization of structural mass and embodied carbon. However, in pin-jointed spatial trusses carrying purely axial forces, material volume and embodied carbon typically exhibit near-linear empirical correlation ($R^2 = 0.81 - 0.97$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:61 -->). When two objective functions lack genuine mechanical opposition, multi-objective Pareto fronts collapse to a narrow locus, rendering artificial multi-objective formulations redundant.
3. **The Decarbonization Paradox in Bio-Structural Assemblies:** A pervasive claim in sustainable architecture asserts that bamboo buildings are intrinsically "carbon-neutral" or "carbon-negative." In whole buildings, life cycle analyses frequently aggregate timber and bamboo materials while omitting connection detailing. However, in large-span spatial domes spanning 40 m<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:48 -->, thousands of member ends converge at spatial hubs. If each member requires steel sleeves, gusset plates, high-tensile bolts, and dense cementitious grout, the cumulative embodied carbon of these metallic and mineral connector components may challenge the green credentials of the bio-based frame. We term this phenomenon the *Decarbonization Paradox*: optimizing the biological members reduces culm dead weight, yet the total carbon footprint remains constrained by the emissions floor imposed by hybrid connection hardware.

## 1.4. Objectives of This Study

To address these gaps, this study investigates single-objective discrete sizing optimization and cradle-to-gate embodied carbon dynamics for three 40.0 m<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:48 --> single-layer spatial domes (Ribbed, Kiewitt, and Geodesic). The specific objectives are:
1. Implement a unified finite element and discrete sizing optimization framework enforcing ISO 22156:2021 / IStructE (2025)<!-- [REF: Trujillo et al. 2025] --> ASD criteria (axial tension, Ylinen column buckling, geometric slenderness $\lambda \le 150$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 -->, connection allowable load $F_{j,Rd} = 49.3\text{ kN}$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:51 -->, and service deflection $u_{\max} \le \text{Span}/150$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:50 -->) under ASCE 7-22 load combinations.
2. Compare the computational performance of three metaheuristic algorithms—Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Dandelion Optimizer (DO; Zhao et al., 2022<!-- [REF NEEDED: Zhao et al. 2022] -->)—across 30 independent seeds (270 total campaign runs<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:57 -->) under strict NFE budgets ($1000 \times N_g$<!-- 00_System/DOCS_UPDATE_PROMPT.md:56 -->) with non-parametric statistical tests (Friedman and Holm-adjusted Mann-Whitney U).
3. Quantify cradle-to-gate (A1–A3) embodied carbon across culm, steel, and mortar components, examining parametric sensitivity to regional supply chains and detailing geometries.
4. Benchmark optimum bamboo domes against equivalent structural steel space domes (a classic 354-member ribbed dome and identical-geometry steel counterparts) in terms of member mass, total system weight, embodied carbon, and material procurement cost across four regional pricing schedules.
5. Identify practical engineering detailing limitations—specifically minimum member length requirements for bolted joint groups—and define transparent boundaries for bio-based spatial grid design.
