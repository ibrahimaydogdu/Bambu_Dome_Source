## 1. Introduction

Large-span spatial structures, such as braced domes and space trusses, provide structural efficiency for covering column-free areas including sports arenas, exhibition centers, and industrial facilities [1, 2]. By transferring external gravity, snow, and wind loads predominantly through axial membrane forces, triangulated geodesic domes achieve high stiffness-to-weight ratios [3, 4]. Conventionally, these systems are constructed using structural steel tubing or aluminum alloys [5]. However, the production of virgin structural steel involves carbon-intensive metallurgical processes, resulting in an embodied carbon emission factor ranging from 1.80 to 2.45 kg $CO_2e$/kg according to inventory of carbon and energy databases [6, 7]. Given that the global building and construction sector accounts for approximately 37% of global operational and process-related greenhouse gas emissions [8], replacing energy-intensive structural metals with fast-growing, bio-based structural materials offers a direct route toward deep structural decarbonization [9, 10].

Structural bamboo (specifically giant timber species such as *Guadua angustifolia* and *Phyllostachys edulis*) functions as a naturally optimized functionally graded composite [11, 12]. Bamboo culms achieve rapid maturation in 3 to 5 years and sequester biogenic atmospheric carbon during cultivation, storing approximately 1.30 kg $CO_2e$ per kg of dry biomass [13, 14]. Structurally, the outer fibrous parenchyma sheath yields a high tensile strength parallel to the grain, often exceeding 100 MPa, while maintaining an elastic modulus ($E_{0,mean}$) between 10,000 and 18,000 MPa at an average bulk density of only 600 to 800 kg/m$^3$ [15, 16]. Consequently, bamboo exhibits a strength-to-weight ratio comparable to, or exceeding, mild structural steel [17].

Despite these structural and environmental attributes, the engineered implementation of bamboo in spatial grid systems introduces pronounced mechanical challenges [18, 19]:
1. **Geometric Non-uniformity and Hollow Culm Cross-Sections:** Naturally grown bamboo culms exhibit variable outer diameters ($D$) and wall thicknesses ($t$) along their length, requiring discrete section mapping from standardized commercial grading catalogs [20].
2. **Buckling and Slenderness Sensitivity:** Due to the hollow thin-walled geometry and moderate longitudinal modulus of elasticity, bamboo compressive elements are prone to flexural and local buckling [21]. Standard design guidelines enforce strict slenderness limits ($\lambda = L_e / r \le 150$) to prevent sudden elastic instability [22].
3. **Connection Vulnerability:** Bamboo possesses low shear and transverse tensile strength across the radial culm wall, rendering direct mechanical fastening susceptible to longitudinal splitting [23]. To mobilize adequate member capacities in spatial domes, engineered joints typically utilize steel connecting plates, threaded rods, and mortar-injected culm ends [24, 25]. Because steel sleeves and cementitious grouts exhibit substantially higher embodied carbon intensities than raw bamboo, connection hardware contributes heavily to the total cradle-to-gate impact of the assembly [26].

To standardize structural safety verification, the International Organization for Standardization released **ISO 22156:2021** (*Bamboo structures — Structural design*) [22], complemented by the comprehensive design guidelines in the **IStructE (2025) Manual** [27]. The standard is fundamentally rooted in Allowable Stress Design (ASD), where allowable member capacities are calculated from characteristic values modified by duration of load ($C_{DF}$), redundancy ($C_R$), temperature ($C_T$), and a safety factor ($FS = 2.0$) without load factoring [22, 27]. Designing large-scale spatial grid systems within ISO 22156 ASD provisions requires iterative finite element structural analysis under governing unfactored load combinations (prescribed by ASCE 7-22 §2.4.1 ASD) [28].

Structural sizing optimization of braced dome systems has been extensively investigated via metaheuristic algorithms [29-32]. Classic benchmark configurations, most notably the 40-meter span 354-member braced dome originally formulated by Saka [33], have served as reference testbeds. However, the original 354-member steel mesh features unbraced lengths between 2.9 and 5.97 m, which severely violate permissible slenderness and compressive stability criteria for natural bamboo culms. To make bio-structural dome optimization viable, a frequency-2 subdivision yielding 1464 members (lengths between 0.76 and 3.08 m) must be derived [33]. Prior investigations in spatial dome optimization exhibit three major shortcomings:
- **Material Exclusivity:** Nearly all benchmark studies assume standardized AISC or Eurocode 3 structural steel pipe profiles, neglecting bio-based structural materials and the standardized ASD design constraints of ISO 22156 [33-37].
- **The Decarbonization Blindspot:** Prior studies often overlook the lifecycle environmental footprint of bio-structures or attempt artificial multi-objective Pareto formulations that ignore physical monotonicity in axial trusses. Crucially, existing literature fails to investigate the parametric sensitivity of the dominant embodied carbon contribution of necessary hybrid steel and mortar connection hardware [26, 38].
- **Absence of Codified Bio-Structural Automation:** While metaheuristic optimization frameworks exist for conventional steel domes [39], automated computational workflows integrating standardized bio-structural ASD design provisions with comprehensive stochastic metaheuristic benchmarking across multiple topologies remain unaddressed.

To bridge these gaps, this study presents a rigorous single-objective structural optimization and cradle-to-gate (A1–A3) lifecycle decarbonization framework for large-span bamboo spatial domes under ISO 22156 / IStructE 2025 ASD constraints. The 40-meter spatial dome benchmark is evaluated across three structural topologies (geodesic16, kiewitt32, and ribbed40) using structural *Guadua angustifolia* culms with engineered mortar-injected steel node connectors. Due to empirical quasi-collinearity between structural mass and embodied carbon in axial truss mechanics ($R^2 = 0.81 - 0.97$) and the absence of an antagonistic physical trade-off mechanism, the problem is formulated as a single-objective penalized weight minimization, followed by post-optimization LCA carbon breakdown and parametric sensitivity across all components.

The principal contributions of this work are threefold:
1. **Codified Bio-Structural ASD Optimization Formulation:** Full integration of ISO 22156:2021 and IStructE (2025) ASD member sizing constraints (axial tension $f_{t,k} = 40\text{ MPa}$, Ylinen column stability with $c = 0.8$, $C_{\text{bow}} = 1 - b_0/0.02$, $b_0 = 0.01$ assumption, $K = 1.0$, slenderness limit $\lambda \le 150$ assumption, joint capacity $F_{j,Rd} = 53.2\text{ kN}$ based on Correal et al. 2021 and $n_{\text{bolt}} = 2$ assumption, and SLS deflection $u_{\max} \le \text{span}/150$) into a discrete cross-sectional mapping scheme using a 24-section Guadua catalog.
2. **Uncovering the Decarbonization Paradox:** Proving numerically and through parametric sensitivity that in large-span bio-composite spatial domes, unavoidable steel node connectors and mortar infill dominate the total cradle-to-gate embodied carbon balance, demonstrating that decarbonization gains are constrained by joint hardware morphology.
3. **Stochastic Metaheuristic Benchmark (3 Algorithms, 30 Seeds):** Comparative evaluation of 3 tuned metaheuristic algorithms ([TUNING SONUCUNA GÖRE]) across 30 independent stochastic runs (seeds 1001–1030) with equal computational budgets (NFE = $1000 \times N_{\text{vars}}$), rigorously validated via two-sided Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon rank-sum (Mann-Whitney U) and seed-blocked Friedman non-parametric hypothesis tests.
